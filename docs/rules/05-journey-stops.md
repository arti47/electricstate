# The Journey and the Stop (GM)

A **Journey** is the campaign. A **Stop** is the adventure — a walkable area the Travelers cannot immediately leave. Leaving a Stop usually ends the session.

## Preparing a Journey
Players may join steps 1–7; steps 8–10 are the GM's alone.
1. Starting Point. 2. Destination. 3. Route. 4. Number of Stops. 5. Each Traveler's personal Goal. 6. Each Traveler's personal Threat. 7. Vehicle. 8. Personal Threat Countdowns. 9. Stops. 10. A few minor Encounters.

| Length | Stops |
|---|---|
| One-shot | 1 |
| Short | 2–4 |
| Medium | 4–7 |
| Long | 8–12 |

One Stop ≈ 1–3 sessions. Length is declared up front so players can pace development and relationships. In a one-shot every personal Threat lands at once; long Journeys spread them so each Traveler gets a spotlight Stop. Journeys can also be destination-less roaming.

Personal Goals: reflect the Dream, be specific, usually tied to the Destination, need not be shared. Each has a **Kicker** — the precipitating event. Also settle *why the group shares a car* (D6 table: gas money, stranded together, only available car, favour to a relative, safety in numbers, owner + hitchhikers).

Personal Threats connect to the Goal and have GM-only Countdowns (typically 3 steps). Travelers with similar Goals may share a Threat.

## Starting and ending
Start: describe/negotiate location, heading and route; ask who drives, where everyone sits, what music is on; hand out Goals (optionally in secret); each player answers appearance, how they met the others, what they've disclosed, how they feel, what they're doing; set Tension; roleplay; optionally start a Countdown.

End: after the final Stop, players narrate reaching the Destination collaboratively. Optional epilogue — each player rolls 3 base dice, each die is a life event whose value sets its fortune, ordered and narrated in turn.

## Anatomy of a Stop
- **Setting** — D6 tables for terrain, population, communications (also how many neurocaster terminals), size, prosperity, weather. Plus D66 mood tables of Electric State imagery and '90s nostalgia.
- **Blocker** — the reason they stop and stay. *Hard* (impassable) or *soft* (a hook tied to a Dream, Flaw or Goal). D66 table. Table understanding: players should not immediately try to unblock it. New groups do better with hard Blockers.
- **Need** — hunger, safety, repairs, medicine, shelter, information; pushes the Travelers into NPC contact. Watch for new needs during play.
- **The Situation** — the backstory and the live conflict, often between rival factions. D66 conflict generator: roll for two parties and one subject.
- **Countdown** — the escalation schedule. Build it on *closing distance and increasing harm*: each step brings the danger nearer (unknown NPCs → known NPCs → loved NPCs → the Travelers) and raises the stakes. Element menu: Presentation, Attack, Lies, Forces gather, Victims appear, Accusation, Plead for help, Sabotage, Someone is captured, A deal is offered, Missing item, A location is seized, Tactics change, Loyalties shift, An alliance is formed.
- **Locations** — key sites plus a map (roads, key buildings, terrain, borders, neurocaster terminals, food/shelter, parking). Show the map; never mark secrets on it. D66 location table.
- **Threats** — see [06-threats.md](06-threats.md). Everyone else is a minor NPC (victims, helpers, informants).

**Past sins**: when leaving a Stop, note NPCs worth reprising as recurring Threats. Never announce it; alter a detail each time they return.

## Running a Stop
Session open: review Tension; set the scene on the road (landscape, weather, season, time, smells, sounds, traffic, needs, road and business names, odd details); roleplay a car scene; reveal the Blocker; let them explore.

At any moment the GM picks one of: sit back and react; activate a Threat or Countdown step; have an NPC pass a rumour; have an NPC ask for or offer help; point out a need; cut to a new scene.

Pacing: activate when players stall, get confused, or want to skip ahead — never while they are engaged, interacting, describing, or executing a plan. Over-activation is the common failure mode.

- **NPC reactions** 2D6: 2 hostile, 3–5 unfriendly, 6–8 neutral, 9–11 friendly, 12 eager to help.
- **Combat morale** 2D6 (roll when an NPC sees an ally fall): 2 flee, 3 surrender, 4–5 retreat or negotiate, 6–12 keep fighting.
- Improvise from the prep, not from a desired outcome; play the preparations and see what happens.
- Escalate world decay across the Journey: near-normal at the first Stop, deserted cities and flickering screens near the end.
- Craft tips: few sharp details, a theme per location, ask players questions, add a contradictory twist, '90s nostalgia, Electric State tech, voices, illustrations, leave horror unexplained, name everything, use music.

## Neuroscapes in play
Only frame a scene inside a neuroscape when a Traveler explores or interacts with avatars — pure information/hacking rolls need no scene. Keep them short; the other players are idle. Generate with three D6 rolls: **Type** (major global → deserted local with a bad reputation), **Theme** (Ancient Rome, Heaven, beach party, space station, Stone Age, deep sea), **Mood** (euphoria, mystery, chill, indulgence, nostalgia, surreal).

## Kicker examples (p. 115)

- Gangsters raid the Traveler's community and burn it to the ground.
- A sibling missing for months makes contact asking for help.
- The Traveler finds a corpse in a car loaded with bags of neurine.
- A revolutionary leader tells the Traveler it is time to strike back.

## Why stick together? (D6, p. 115)

| D6 | Reason |
|---|---|
| 1 | To split gas money. |
| 2 | You were all stranded together at a bus station. |
| 3 | It is the only car available where the Journey starts. |
| 4 | A relative or friend asked if the others could come along as a favor. |
| 5 | Only a fool travels this road alone and without guns. |
| 6 | One of you owns the car and the others are hitchhikers. |
