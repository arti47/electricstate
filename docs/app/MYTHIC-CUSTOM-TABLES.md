# Comprehensive Report: Custom Elements Meaning Tables
*Based on Mythic Magazine Volume 38*

---

## I. Overview & Philosophy

Elements Meaning Tables expand upon Mythic’s core Action and Description tables by providing targeted, thematic word lists for specific scenarios. Constructing your own custom tables allows you to fine-tune the randomized results to perfectly fit the tone, setting, and specific needs of your adventure. 

### Why Build Your Own Tables?
*   **Faster Interpretations:** Custom tables generate words already aligned with your current context, making on-the-fly interpretation much faster and easier.
*   **Targeted Themes:** They cover niche subjects not squarely represented by standard tables (e.g., "Mutated Animals" for a post-apocalyptic setting, or "Funky Pirate Ship Names" for a nautical campaign).
*   **Creative Engagement:** The act of building the table allows you to think deeply about your adventure's core themes.

### The Power of One Word
Mythic’s Meaning Tables use a single word per entry to maximize utility:
1.  **Simplicity & Speed:** A single word is processed faster by the brain during gameplay.
2.  **Unbiased Interpretation:** A single word floats untethered in your imagination, attaching itself to the first logical concept. By contrast, a two-word phrase (e.g., "Crocodile Soldier") is too specific. Breaking it down into "Reptile," "Soldier," or "Military" allows for infinitely more combinations when paired with other rolls.

## II. The 5-Step Table Creation Process

Building a 100-word table may seem daunting, but it follows a structured, iterative process.

### Step 1: Define Your Subject
Determine exactly what the table is for. Decide how specific it needs to be based on its intended use:
*   **Broad:** *Tropical Island Descriptors* (e.g., Deserted, Forested, Ruins)
*   **Narrow:** *Treasure Chest Descriptors* (e.g., Gold, Weapons, Trap)
*   **Highly Specific:** *Funky Pirate Ship Names* (e.g., Red, Kraken, Doom)

### Step 2: Brainstorm Initial Words (The "Brain Dump")
Start "cold"—without outside research. Open a text document and type every word that comes to mind regarding your subject. Do not edit, filter, or overthink. Capture the "low-hanging fruit" first (e.g., for a tropical island: sand, water, tree, coconut, storm). 

When the flow slows down, look at your existing words to find synonyms or opposites to push your ideas further.

### Step 3: Branch Out (Research)
Once your personal well of ideas runs dry, seek outside inspiration to avoid limiting the table strictly to your own biases. Search online for images or articles related to your subject (e.g., searching "Pirate ship names" to find historical examples like *Revenge* or *Royal Fortune*, leading to words like Vengeance, Noble, or Retribution).

### Step 4: Add Neutral Words
Inject broadly applicable words into your list. Neutral words act as flexible modifiers for your highly targeted words. 
*   **Action-Oriented:** General verbs and subjects (e.g., Assist, Betray, Power).
*   **Description-Oriented:** General adjectives (e.g., Abnormal, Beautiful, Dirty).
*   *Note:* Highly specific tables (like "Smells" or "Scavenging") require fewer neutral words, but almost all tables benefit from at least a few.

### Step 5: Edit, Refine, and Reduce to 100
Sort your massive list of words alphabetically to easily spot duplicates and group concepts.
*   **Cut Neutral Words:** Remove any general words that clash with your setting.
*   **Adjust Specificity:** Decide if a word is too narrow (change "Banana" to "Fruit") or too broad (change "Object" to "Wreckage").
*   **Synonym City:** If you want a specific outcome to happen more frequently (e.g., bar fights in a tavern), add multiple synonyms for that concept (Fight, Brawl, Melee, Fisticuffs) instead of listing the same word twice.
*   **The Final Cut:** Prioritize your 30–50 most vital, targeted words, and fill the rest of the 100 slots with the best-fitting neutral words.

## III. Special Mechanics & Modifiers

### The "Anything Words"
These ten words are universally applicable modifiers that indicate a shift in state or a disruption of expectations. It is highly recommended to include some or all of these in your custom tables:

> **Change, Continue, Decrease, Increase, Mundane, Mysterious, Start, Stop, Strange, Extra**

*   **Change / Continue / Start / Stop:** Ideal for action tables to indicate a shift in a character's state or behavior (e.g., "Start" + "Prison" = NPC is captured).
*   **Decrease / Increase / Extra:** Modifiers for size, quantity, or intensity (e.g., "Decrease" + "Intimidating" = A villain's influence is waning).
*   **Mundane:** Grounds the narrative by enforcing an ordinary, expected state, balancing out the more extreme words on the table.
*   **Mysterious:** Injects the unknown, prompting an action or description that has hidden motives.
*   **Strange:** The "X-Factor." This signals the player to think outside the box and invent a wildly unexpected interpretation (e.g., "Strange" + "Environment" = floating rocks; "Strange" + "Damaged" = a melted gun at a standard crime scene).

### Doubling Down (Double Results)
When rolling on a single d100 table multiple times, you may roll the exact same word twice (e.g., "Decrease Decrease"). This should be interpreted as an extreme amplification of that concept.
*   *Example:* A scout rolling a single "Decrease" might mean an enemy force is half its expected size. Rolling "Decrease Decrease" means the camp is entirely abandoned save for one person.

> *"The difference between the almost right word and the right word is really a large matter. 'tis the difference between the lightning bug and the lightning."* — Mark Twain
